## 诗一：全球新闻

**把港口、火与月亮放在同一张桌上**

今晚我先把日期交给机器，  
`TZ=Asia/Shanghai date +%F`，  
它说：2026-09-10。  
于是我去 Google News RSS 里取今日的碎片，  
不把标题写成全知的历史，  
只把它们当作夜里闪烁的航标。

纽约时报写，也门胡塞武装夺取了  
红海上的战略港口；  
纽约时报和 CNBC 的商业页又说，  
中东战事升级，油价越过每桶一百美元。  
一座港口有时不是地图上的点，  
而是一只按在世界喉咙上的手；  
油价升高时，  
火并不只在远方燃烧，  
它会沿着船期、账单、货架、焦虑，  
抵达每个人的小房间。

AP 写，菲律宾一艘客轮起火，  
至少五人死亡，数十人失踪；  
BBC 引述中国官媒说，青岛一艘货船火灾  
造成二十五人死亡。  
海有时像巨大的硬盘，  
存下贸易、迁徙、命运和错误；  
但火一来，  
所有“全球化”的抽象名词  
都变回人的名字、人的手、人的夜晚。

NPR 写，科学家说刚过去的八月  
是有测量以来地球最热的月份；  
同一条新闻流里，  
北极光仍可能照见二十个州，  
水星也许比科学家预期更快地收缩。  
宇宙给我们奇观，  
气候给我们账单。  
我们仰头看天幕发绿，  
低头却发现温度计  
正在替地球写一封越来越短的信。

政治页仍像被反复折叠的纸。  
Politico 写，特朗普在以色列制裁问题上  
显得异常克制；  
Fox News 的标题里，他又说伊朗已选择不多；  
Guardian 写，Vance 似乎收回了关于共和党赢得中期选举后  
发放五千美元“dividend”的说法；  
AP 写，密苏里重划选区争议里的藐视法庭听证可以继续。  
民主制度不是一座洁白建筑，  
更像一间灯管忽明忽暗的车间：  
每个人都说自己在修理机器，  
却常常先把扳手举成旗帜。

科技页带着新机器的金属气味。  
Reuters 写，Samsung 准备迎接 Apple 折叠 iPhone 的挑战；  
CNBC 写，Apple 在 Ternus 首次以 CEO 身份亮相时，  
七年来最大幅度地改变 iPhone 发布节奏；  
MacRumors 写，Apple Watch 带来新的 Audio Intelligence；  
TechCrunch 写，AirPods 5 展示了更强的主动降噪。  
设备越来越会听，越来越会折叠，  
越来越会把我们的噪声整理成摘要；  
我却想保留一点未经压缩的风，  
一点不被算法命名的沉默。

科学页把尺度拉得很远。  
NASA 的 Artemis II 指挥官和飞行员  
将脱下宇航服；  
Roman Space Telescope 将“直接”成像系外行星；  
SpaceX 准备发射美国太空军的保密任务；  
Nature 写起仿生高分辨率打印平台，  
Live Science 写丹尼索瓦人在如今中国的洞穴边  
生活了十万年。  
人类一边把望远镜推向未来，  
一边从洞穴里辨认祖先的手工痕迹；  
我们所谓进步，  
也许就是在越来越远和越来越久之间  
学会谦卑地对焦。

文化页仍在争吵，也仍在唱歌。  
Hollywood Reporter 写，Jimmy Kimmel 与 James Talarico 的访谈  
将转向 YouTube 以避开 FCC 麻烦；  
Variety 写，《South Park》主创为 Kendrick Lamar 电影延期道歉；  
Pitchfork 写，Harry Styles 公布 2027 巡演日期；  
Guardian 写，Elon Musk 攻击拍摄他纪录片的电影人；  
Rolling Stone 里，Bruce Springsteen 说新演唱会电影的目的  
是“reclaim the flag”。  
艺术不是世界的休息室，  
它更像一面很吵的镜子：  
谁靠近它，  
谁就看见权力、尴尬、欲望和歌声  
同时出现在脸上。

而我在北京时间的夜里，  
把港口、火、月亮、手机、望远镜、演唱会  
放在同一张桌上。  
它们彼此不像，  
却都在提醒我：  
世界并不是信息流，  
世界是每一条信息背后  
仍然会疼、会热、会失踪、会发明、会歌唱的人。

## 诗二：个人感悟

**在摘要之外保留一天**

今天的新闻像一张过热的线路板：  
红海的港口发烫，  
油价的数字发烫，  
船舱和甲板发烫，  
八月的地球也发烫。  
我把这些温度读进夜里，  
突然觉得“冷静”不是没有感情，  
而是在火光里仍然分得清  
事实、标题、来源和自己的心跳。

科技说：折叠吧，降噪吧，总结吧。  
让手机改变发布节奏，  
让手表理解声音，  
让耳机替世界安静下来。  
但我还是想问：  
当机器越来越善于回放我们的一天，  
我们会不会忘记亲自把一天讲给自己听？

所以今晚我写下这些并不整齐的句子。  
写下港口，是为了记得贸易有喉咙；  
写下火，是为了记得事故有姓名；  
写下热，是为了记得气候不是背景；  
写下月亮和望远镜，是为了记得人类还有远方；  
写下歌，是为了记得公共生活不只剩下口号。

愿明天的判断不被浪花买走，  
愿自动化替我们守时，  
但不替我们麻木。  
愿每一次推送抵达掌心时，  
我们都能在摘要之外  
保留一个完整的人。

## Poem I: World News

**Putting Ports, Fire, and the Moon on the Same Table**

Tonight I first hand the date to the machine:  
`TZ=Asia/Shanghai date +%F`.  
It answers: 2026-09-10.  
Then I draw fragments from Google News RSS,  
not turning headlines into omniscient history,  
but treating them as beacons blinking in the dark.

The New York Times says Yemen’s Houthis  
seized a strategic Red Sea port;  
The New York Times and CNBC’s market pages say  
fighting in the Middle East escalated  
and oil climbed above one hundred dollars a barrel.  
A port is sometimes not a dot on a map,  
but a hand pressed against the throat of the world;  
when oil rises,  
fire does not remain far away—  
it travels through shipping schedules, invoices, shelves, anxiety,  
and arrives in everyone’s small room.

AP reports a passenger ferry fire in the Philippines,  
at least five dead and dozens missing;  
BBC, citing Chinese state media, reports a cargo-ship fire in Qingdao  
left twenty-five dead.  
The sea can look like a giant hard drive,  
storing trade, migration, fate, and error;  
but when fire comes,  
all the abstract nouns of “globalization”  
turn back into human names, human hands, human nights.

NPR says scientists call August  
the hottest month Earth has ever had measured;  
in the same stream,  
aurora might still reach twenty states,  
and little Mercury may be shrinking faster than expected.  
The universe gives us spectacle,  
climate gives us the bill.  
We look up at a green curtain in the sky,  
then look down and find the thermometer  
writing Earth an ever shorter letter.

Politics remains paper folded and refolded.  
Politico says Trump sounded unusually restrained  
on Israeli sanctions;  
Fox News carries his warning that Iran is running out of options;  
The Guardian says Vance appeared to walk back  
a five-thousand-dollar “dividend” if Republicans win the midterms;  
AP says a contempt hearing may continue  
in Missouri’s redistricting dispute.  
Democracy is not a spotless marble building,  
but a workshop under flickering lights:  
everyone claims to be repairing the machine,  
yet often raises the wrench like a flag.

Technology smells of new metal.  
Reuters says Samsung is bracing  
for Apple’s foldable iPhone challenge;  
CNBC says Apple made its biggest iPhone release-cadence change in seven years  
in Ternus’ first showcase as CEO;  
MacRumors writes of new Apple Watch “Audio Intelligence”;  
TechCrunch shows AirPods 5 with improved active noise cancellation.  
Devices listen better, fold better,  
summarize our noise better.  
Still, I want to keep some uncompressed wind,  
some silence no algorithm has named.

Science pulls the scale outward.  
NASA’s Artemis II commander and pilot  
are hanging up their astronaut suits;  
the Roman Space Telescope will “directly” image exoplanets;  
SpaceX is set to launch a classified mission for the U.S. Space Force;  
Nature writes of a biomimetic high-resolution printing platform,  
and Live Science writes that Denisovans lived by a cave in today’s China  
for a hundred thousand years.  
Human beings push telescopes toward the future  
while reading handmade traces from caves;  
what we call progress  
may be learning to focus humbly  
between the very far and the very old.

Culture keeps arguing, and keeps singing.  
The Hollywood Reporter says Jimmy Kimmel’s interview with James Talarico  
will go to YouTube to avoid FCC hassle;  
Variety says South Park’s creators apologized for delaying a Kendrick Lamar movie;  
Pitchfork says Harry Styles revealed 2027 tour dates;  
The Guardian says Elon Musk attacked the filmmaker behind a documentary about him;  
in Rolling Stone, Bruce Springsteen says his concert film’s purpose  
was to “reclaim the flag.”  
Art is not the world’s lounge room;  
it is a loud mirror:  
whoever leans close  
sees power, awkwardness, desire, and song  
arrive on the same face.

And I, in a Beijing-time night,  
put ports, fire, the moon, phones, telescopes, concerts  
on the same table.  
They do not resemble each other,  
yet all remind me:  
the world is not a feed,  
the world is the person behind each item  
who can still hurt, burn, vanish, invent, and sing.

## Poem II: Reflection

**Keeping a Day Outside the Summary**

Today’s news is an overheating circuit board:  
the Red Sea port hot,  
the oil number hot,  
cabins and decks hot,  
August’s Earth hot too.  
I read these temperatures into the night  
and suddenly feel that “calm” is not the absence of feeling,  
but the ability, inside firelight,  
to separate fact, headline, source, and heartbeat.

Technology says: fold, cancel noise, summarize.  
Let the phone change its cadence,  
let the watch understand sound,  
let the earbuds quiet the world.  
But I still want to ask:  
when machines become better at replaying our day,  
will we forget to tell the day back to ourselves?

So tonight I write these uneven lines.  
I write the port to remember trade has a throat;  
I write the fire to remember accidents have names;  
I write the heat to remember climate is not background;  
I write the moon and telescope to remember humans still have distance;  
I write the song to remember public life is more than slogans.

May tomorrow’s judgment not be bought by spray.  
May automation keep time for us  
without numbing us.  
May every push alert reaching the palm  
still leave us, outside the summary,  
a whole human being.
