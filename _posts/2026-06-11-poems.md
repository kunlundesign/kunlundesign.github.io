## 诗一：开幕日

**世界杯，六月十一日**

当足球在墨西哥升起，
三十二面旗帜在风中招展，
我们暂时忘记了
那些更沉重的数字：

伊朗与美国的导弹，
El Niño 带来的热浪，
北爱尔兰燃烧的街道——
全都被欢呼声淹没。

也许这就是体育的意义：
不是逃避，而是暂停，
让人类记起
我们也能为美丽的事物欢呼。

九十分钟的休战，
在哨声响起之前，
全世界都在同一片草地上，
追逐同一个梦。

## 诗二：哈格岛的阴影

**威胁**

特朗普说要"狠狠地打"，
指着伊朗的心脏——
哈格岛，那座浮在波斯湾上的
石油泵站。

"在不久的将来，
我们将完全控制
他们的石油和天然气市场。"
语言如此轻松，像在谈论天气。

而在那些数字背后，
是三个印度水手的生命，
在阿曼附近的海水中熄灭，
成为大国博弈的脚注。

战争总是从威胁开始，
从"如果"和"那么"的句式，
从桌上的拳头
到海峡里的火焰。

我们见证过太多次：
言语变成行动，
行动变成历史，
历史变成尘土。

## 诗三：厄尔尼诺的信

**给明年的天气**

科学家宣布：
El Niño 正式开始，
像一封寄往未来的信，
写满了极端天气的预言。

某处会有洪水，
某处会有干旱，
某处的农田会裂开，
某处的城市会沉没。

我们读着这封信，
知道收件人是我们自己，
却假装它寄给了别人——
那些遥远的、抽象的受害者。

气候变化最残酷的真相：
它总是发生在"那边"，
直到有一天，
"那边"变成了家门口。

## Poem 1: Opening Day

**World Cup, June Eleventh**

As the ball rises in Mexico,
thirty-two flags flutter in the wind,
we briefly forget
those heavier numbers:

Missiles between Iran and America,
heat waves brought by El Niño,
streets burning in Northern Ireland—
all drowned out by cheers.

Perhaps this is what sport means:
not escape, but pause,
reminding humanity
we can also cheer for beautiful things.

A ninety-minute ceasefire,
before the whistle blows,
the whole world on the same pitch,
chasing the same dream.

## Poem 2: Shadow of Kharg Island

**Threats**

Trump says he'll hit them "very hard,"
pointing at Iran's heart—
Kharg Island, that oil pump
floating on the Persian Gulf.

"In the not too distant future,
we will assume total control
of their oil and gas markets."
Words so casual, like discussing weather.

Behind those numbers,
three Indian sailors' lives,
extinguished in waters near Oman,
becoming footnotes to great power games.

War always starts with threats,
with "if" and "then" constructions,
from fists on tables
to flames in straits.

We've witnessed this too many times:
words becoming actions,
actions becoming history,
history becoming dust.

## Poem 3: El Niño's Letter

**To Next Year's Weather**

Scientists announce:
El Niño has officially begun,
like a letter sent to the future,
filled with prophecies of extreme weather.

Somewhere there will be floods,
somewhere there will be drought,
somewhere farmland will crack open,
somewhere cities will sink.

We read this letter,
knowing we are the addressees,
yet pretend it's meant for others—
those distant, abstract victims.

Climate change's cruelest truth:
it always happens "over there,"
until one day,
"over there" becomes our doorstep.
