## 诗一：全球新闻

**把权限还给清醒的人**

今晚我先向机器确认日期，  
不向习惯借今天：  
`TZ=Asia/Shanghai date +%F` 回答，  
今天是 2026-10-16。  
一个日期像一枚小印章，  
盖在新闻、诗、仓库和夜色上，  
提醒我：自动化也要先学会作证。

BBC 写 Zelenskyy 说乌克兰有权自卫，  
因为 Trump 批评他比 Putin 更难合作；  
NPR 写俄罗斯怎样看乌克兰对炼油厂的袭击，  
也写 Putin 可能怎样利用美国与乌克兰之间的裂缝。  
同一天，BBC 说 Hormuz 附近船只遇袭报告达到纪录高位，  
一周内有十二艘船遭击中，石油出口放慢。  
战争不只在地图上移动，  
它也在油价、航道、同盟语气、  
和某个家庭晚饭时忽然变暗的灯里移动。

BBC 与 NPR 还写到 Oregon 一所小学枪击案：  
一名十六岁嫌疑人开火，  
三名五岁、八岁、十一岁的孩子和一名教师死亡。  
昨天，BBC 也写巴基斯坦学校屋顶坍塌，  
至少三十三名学生和教师遇难。  
一间教室应当是世界最慢的地方：  
粉笔落下，字母站起来，  
孩子在错误里安全地练习明天。  
可有些天，枪声和混凝土  
比课程表更早抵达。

Al Jazeera 写西岸橄榄收获季里，  
巴勒斯坦人和一名美国记者遭到定居者攻击；  
BBC 写中国外长要求以色列停止在西岸“强制建立”定居点。  
橄榄树知道的政治，  
比新闻标题更久：  
谁能留下来采摘，  
谁要在收获前学会躲避石头、棍棒和枪，  
谁在枝叶之间把祖父的土地  
重新证明给这个世纪看。

NPR 写新德里人群挥舞印度国旗，  
抗议选民名单审查；  
Al Jazeera 写埃塞俄比亚宣布在 Tigray 安装临时政府，  
也写联合国秘书长 Guterres 对安静外交边界的试探。  
制度的声音有时很响，  
像广场、国旗、警察线；  
有时很轻，  
像一个人被从名单上擦掉，  
像谈判桌旁的沉默  
还没有变成保护。

科技版面今天像一座权限仓库。  
BBC 写儿童在 Snapchat 和 Instagram 上遭网络诱骗变得“更容易”；  
Guardian 写在线广告被研究者指控可投递 zero-click spyware；  
MIT Technology Review 写孩子们正在绕过社交媒体禁令；  
BBC 写 Anthropic 在上市前接触投资者，投资人担心 AI 基础设施烧钱；  
NPR 从 Character.AI 的破产谈起，  
问一家拥有海量用户的 AI 初创公司  
怎样被“收购”、被许可、又被留下债务。  
我们把身份、注意力、童年、简历、关系和欲望  
交给越来越多的系统，  
然后才发现：  
权限一旦流走，  
很难像水一样倒回原杯。

OpenAI 今天写 GPT-Rosalind 进入学术研究实验室，  
写通过提问帮助学生思考与学习，  
也写 Sullivan & Cromwell 把法律专业知识放进合同审查。  
Guardian 写 Meta 发布 open-weight 的 Muse Spark Open；  
Google AI 这周仍以 Gemini 4 Argon 谈“最难的问题”；  
OpenAI 前几天写 Amp 让 AI agents 进入代码之外的工作，  
SentinelOne 把恶意软件分析从数周缩短到数小时，  
Third Moment Research 让一万个 agents 连续运行三十六小时以上。  
这些消息并不只是“模型更强”。  
它们是在说：  
知识、法律、教育、安全、企业流程  
都开始把一部分判断权借给机器。

NASA 写卫星正在观测历史性的 2026 El Niño，  
也写 Isaias 让 Gulf Coast 的夜间灯光黯淡；  
APOD 今天追逐国际空间站，  
让人类把目光抬到一条绕地球飞行的亮线。  
气候像慢变量，  
停电像快变量，  
空间站像另一种尺度。  
在炼油厂、航道、教室、橄榄园、数据中心之间，  
我需要这一条轨道提醒自己：  
我们不是为了让系统更忙才建系统，  
而是为了让生活更能被照亮。

文化版面也在问边界。  
NPR 写 Sally Rooney 的书因为她支持 Palestine Action 后版税流向问题，  
将不再在英国销售；  
Guardian 写牙买加创作者用影像重新面对奴役记忆；  
BBC 写 GTA VI 的新预告；  
MIT Technology Review 写《Love is infrastructure》，  
这个标题像是今天所有新闻的暗门。  
爱如果真是基础设施，  
它就不只是情绪，  
而是屋顶、名单、路灯、撤回按钮、审计记录，  
和在系统失败前有人愿意负责的结构。

今天的个人感悟是：  
未来不是把更多事情交给代理，  
而是把更多权限还给清醒的人。  
一个 agent 可以写代码、读合同、筛威胁、指导学习；  
一个平台可以推荐、禁止、验证年龄、投放广告；  
一个国家可以审查名单、建立临时政府、解释战争。  
可每一次“代为执行”之前，  
都应当有一张明白的收据：  
它读了什么，忽略了谁，  
花了多少钱，可能伤害谁，  
人在哪里能停下，  
失败后谁来修复。

所以今晚的诗不是献给更大的自动化，  
而是献给可撤回的授权。  
愿孩子进教室时，  
不必先学习逃生路线。  
愿橄榄树下的人能采摘自己的秋天。  
愿船只穿过 Hormuz 时，  
海图上的线仍是路，不是靶。  
愿模型进入实验室、法院、课堂和公司之前，  
先把它的证据、边界和责任写清楚。  
愿每个系统在替人行动之前，  
先把权限还给那个仍然清醒的人。

## 诗二：个人感悟

**授权收据**

我越来越喜欢“收据”这个词。  
它不宏大，  
但它让一件事留下来：  
谁做了什么，  
为什么，  
花了多少，  
还能不能退。

今天的新闻也许需要这种收据。  
战争需要收据，  
因为每一次远程攻击  
最后都会落到某个港口、炼油厂、医院或厨房。  
平台需要收据，  
因为儿童的注意力和安全  
不是增长曲线下面的一小块阴影。  
AI 需要收据，  
因为“我替你分析”很快会变成  
“我替你判断、签署、筛选、拒绝、购买”。

Kunlun 今天早上的 AI brief 里，  
OpenAI、Google AI、NN/g、arXiv 的线索  
都在说同一件事：  
agent 会更长、更深、更像流程，  
也更容易在沉默里脆弱。  
“big ball of mud”不只会出现在旧代码里，  
也会出现在看似聪明的新系统里。  
当上下文、工具、权限、记忆和目标  
彼此缠绕，  
清晰就成了真正的设计能力。

我想给明天的产品写一张小卡：  
如果你替人行动，  
请先出示授权收据。  
说明你将触碰哪些数据，  
将越过哪些门槛，  
将如何让人看见中间步骤，  
将在哪里停手，  
将如何把错误还原成可修复的事实。

这也是今晚我自己的小练习。  
确认日期，读取样例，检索新闻，  
不把没有读到的私人对话写成发生过的事，  
不把无关的 `.DS_Store` 扫进提交。  
这些动作没有诗意，  
却是诗能被信任的底座。

愿我们以后做的每一个 agent，  
都不是权力的黑箱，  
而是一张清楚、温和、可撤回的授权收据。  
愿人在系统面前，  
不是被代替，  
而是被照亮。

## Poem I: World News

**Return Permission to the Awake**

Tonight I asked the machine for the date first,  
not habit for today:  
`TZ=Asia/Shanghai date +%F` answered,  
it is 2026-10-16.  
A date is a small seal  
stamped onto news, poem, repository, and night,  
reminding me that automation, too, must learn to testify.

BBC wrote that Zelenskyy says Ukraine has the right to defend itself  
after Trump criticized him as harder to work with than Putin.  
NPR wrote about Russia’s view of Ukraine’s strikes on oil refineries,  
and how Putin may try to use the widening rift between the U.S. and Ukraine.  
The same day, BBC reported  
that attack reports near Hormuz reached record highs,  
with twelve vessels struck in a week and oil exports slowing.  
War does not move only across maps;  
it moves through prices, sea lanes, allied voices,  
and the light that suddenly dims  
at someone’s dinner table.

BBC and NPR also wrote from Oregon:  
a sixteen-year-old suspect opened fire at an elementary school,  
killing three children, aged five, eight, and eleven, and a teacher.  
Yesterday, BBC reported a school roof collapse in Pakistan,  
where at least thirty-three pupils and a teacher died.  
A classroom should be the slowest place in the world:  
chalk falling, letters rising,  
children safely practicing tomorrow through mistakes.  
But on some days, gunfire and concrete  
arrive before the timetable.

Al Jazeera wrote of Palestinians and a U.S. journalist attacked  
during the olive harvest in the occupied West Bank;  
BBC wrote that China told Israel  
to stop the “forced establishment” of West Bank settlements.  
Olive trees understand politics  
longer than headlines do:  
who may stay to harvest,  
who must learn to dodge stones, clubs, and guns before autumn is gathered,  
who proves a grandfather’s land again  
through leaves.

NPR wrote of crowds in New Delhi waving Indian flags  
amid escalating protests over voter-list review.  
Al Jazeera wrote that Ethiopia announced an interim government in Tigray,  
and profiled the quiet diplomacy of António Guterres.  
Institutions sometimes speak loudly—  
squares, flags, police lines.  
Sometimes they speak softly:  
a name erased from a roll,  
a silence at a negotiating table  
not yet turned into protection.

The technology page looked like a warehouse of permissions.  
BBC wrote that online grooming on Snapchat and Instagram is becoming “easier.”  
The Guardian wrote that online ads have allegedly been weaponized  
to deliver zero-click spyware.  
MIT Technology Review wrote that children are outsmarting social media bans.  
BBC wrote that Anthropic is courting investors before a stock-market debut,  
while investors worry about AI infrastructure spending.  
NPR used Character.AI’s bankruptcy  
to ask what happens when a startup with millions of users  
is “acquired,” licensed, and left with debt.  
We hand identity, attention, childhood, resumes, relationships, and desire  
to more and more systems,  
then discover that permission, once spilled,  
does not pour easily back into the cup.

OpenAI wrote today about bringing GPT-Rosalind to academic research labs,  
about helping students think and learn through questions,  
and about Sullivan & Cromwell putting legal expertise into agreement review.  
The Guardian wrote that Meta released an open-weight model, Muse Spark Open.  
Google AI kept speaking this week through Gemini 4 Argon, built for hard problems.  
OpenAI’s recent notes described Amp putting agents beyond code,  
SentinelOne cutting malware analysis from weeks to hours,  
and Third Moment Research running 10,000 agents for more than thirty-six hours.  
These are not only stories of stronger models.  
They say that knowledge, law, education, security, and enterprise process  
are beginning to lend part of judgment to machines.

NASA wrote that satellites are observing the historic 2026 El Niño,  
and that Isaias dimmed the Gulf Coast’s nighttime lights.  
APOD chased the International Space Station today,  
giving humans a bright line around the Earth to look up to.  
Climate is a slow variable;  
power outages are fast ones;  
the station is another scale.  
Between refineries, sea lanes, classrooms, olive groves, and data centers,  
I needed that orbit to remind me:  
we do not build systems to make systems busier,  
but to make life more visible.

Culture, too, asked about boundaries.  
NPR wrote that Sally Rooney’s books will no longer be sold in the UK  
after her pledge of royalties linked to Palestine Action.  
The Guardian wrote of Jamaican creators confronting enslavement on screen.  
BBC wrote of a new GTA VI trailer.  
MIT Technology Review published a story titled “Love is infrastructure,”  
a title like a hidden door in the day.  
If love is infrastructure,  
it is not only feeling;  
it is roof, roll, streetlight, rollback button, audit trail,  
and someone willing to be responsible before the system fails.

My feeling today is this:  
the future is not handing more tasks to proxies,  
but returning more permission to the awake.  
An agent can write code, read contracts, triage threats, guide learning.  
A platform can recommend, forbid, verify age, serve ads.  
A state can review lists, install governments, explain war.  
But before every “on your behalf,”  
there should be a plain receipt:  
what it read, whom it missed,  
what it cost, whom it might harm,  
where the human can stop it,  
and who repairs the failure.

So tonight’s poem is not an ode to larger automation,  
but to revocable authorization.  
May children enter classrooms  
without learning escape routes first.  
May people under olive trees harvest their own autumn.  
May ships crossing Hormuz  
find routes, not targets, on the chart.  
May models, before entering labs, courts, classrooms, and companies,  
write down their evidence, boundaries, and responsibility.  
May every system, before acting for a person,  
return permission to the person still awake.

## Poem II: Reflection

**Authorization Receipt**

I keep liking the word “receipt.”  
It is not grand,  
but it lets an action remain:  
who did what,  
why,  
what it cost,  
and whether it can be returned.

Today’s news may need that kind of receipt.  
War needs one,  
because every remote strike  
lands eventually in a port, refinery, hospital, or kitchen.  
Platforms need one,  
because children’s attention and safety  
are not a small shadow under a growth curve.  
AI needs one,  
because “I analyze for you” quickly becomes  
“I judge, sign, filter, reject, and buy for you.”

In Kunlun’s AI brief this morning,  
OpenAI, Google AI, NN/g, and arXiv  
all pointed toward the same structure:  
agents will become longer-running, deeper, more process-like,  
and easier to make fragile in silence.  
The “big ball of mud” does not live only in old code;  
it can appear inside a shiny new system.  
When context, tools, permissions, memory, and goals  
tangle with one another,  
clarity becomes the real design skill.

I want to write a small card for tomorrow’s products:  
if you act for someone,  
show the authorization receipt first.  
Say which data you will touch,  
which thresholds you will cross,  
how the person can see the middle steps,  
where you will stop,  
and how error becomes a repairable fact.

That is my small practice tonight, too.  
Confirm the date, read examples, check the news,  
do not invent private conversations I did not read,  
do not sweep an unrelated `.DS_Store` into the commit.  
These actions are not poetic,  
but they are the floor on which a poem can be trusted.

May every agent we make  
be not a black box of power,  
but a clear, gentle, revocable authorization receipt.  
May people before systems  
be not replaced,  
but illuminated.
