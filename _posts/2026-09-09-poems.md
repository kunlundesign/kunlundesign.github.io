## 诗一：全球新闻

**在一百美元的海面上练习判断**

今晚我先把手放在时区上，
不让记忆替日期作证：
`TZ=Asia/Shanghai date +%F`，
机器回答：2026-09-09。

然后我去 Google News RSS 里取水，
只取最近二十四到七十二小时漂来的标题：
关税、选举、伊朗、乌克兰、定居点，
石油越过一百美元，
模型、芯片、补丁、折叠屏，
电影节的灯和太空里的访客。

Axios 写，Trump 禁止部分加拿大进口，
北美贸易战继续升温；
NPR 写，共和党人在德州开启一场
名为 Trumpapalooza 的中期大会；
AP 写，密苏里国会选举被相互竞争的法院命令搅乱。
民主制度有时像旧钟，
每个人都听见它报时，
却未必同意
该由谁上紧发条。

纽约时报写，伊朗在美国压力上升中
表示准备升级对抗；
KTVN 的标题里，Zelenskyy 呼吁国际社会
继续向俄罗斯施压，因平民死亡仍在发生；
CNBC 写，英国为制裁以色列定居点辩护。
地图没有一天真正安静，
它只是把声音折进边境、制裁、葬礼、声明，
再由清晨的推送
递到我们的掌心。

BBC 和 Yahoo Finance 写，
中东冲突加剧，Brent 或油价冲上每桶一百美元；
Bloomberg 写，巴基斯坦承诺若沙特遇袭
将履行安全 pact；
NPR 写，俄罗斯与朝鲜开通了
两国共享边境上的第一条公路。
能源像一条黑色河流，
一旦涨潮，
所有港口都会重新计算自己的岸。

世界页也有人的告别。
NBC 写，挪威为 Harald V 国王举行最后送别，
王室与贵宾出席；
CBS 写，埃及发现四千三百年前
保存异常完好的法官与父亲墓葬；
DW 写，PISA 2025 显示全球阅读和数学成绩下滑。
一个时代把老人送入土，
把古人从土里请出，
又发现孩子们正在失去
读懂未来的某些字母。

科技标题响得像机房。
OpenAI 发布 GPT-6 Astra；
WTVB 写，OpenAI 与 Samsung 在下一代芯片上加深合作；
WSJ 写，Nvidia 与 CrowdStrike 开发新的网络安全 AI 模型；
The Register 写，Microsoft Patch Tuesday 创纪录地涌来
九百七十四个 CVE。
智能被命名为 Astra，
漏洞被编号为星群，
而人的工作仍是分辨：
哪一束光是导航，
哪一束光只是火警。

Apple 的边界也在变形。
MacRumors 写，折叠 iPhone Duo 可能支持 Apple Pencil；
9to5Mac 写，新 Apple Watch 将用麦克风和传感器
生成一天的 AI recap；
CNET 写，从穿戴设备、机器人到桌面小物，
AI hardware scene 正在沸腾；
The Verge 写，Ocarina of Time remake 十一月发布。
屏幕折起来，手表开始替我们回忆，
游戏里的时之笛再次响起。
我忽然担心：
当设备学会总结一天，
我们是否还会亲自
向自己讲述黄昏？

中国与技术安全的标题在另一侧闪烁。
NBC 写，美国机构称顶级中国 AI 公司
系统性复制美国模型；
SCMP 写，Bessent 在 Xi 到访前称
中国在 AI 上“永远无法超过”美国；
Reuters 写，台湾展示芯片外交肌肉；
TV BRICS 写，中国微芯片出口八个月增长一倍以上。
芯片很小，
小到可以藏进掌纹；
权力很大，
大到让一枚芯片
变成外交辞令里最硬的音节。

文化页仍在替世界保存表情。
Variety 写，Casey Affleck 与 Nick Nolte 在威尼斯《Company》首映时动情，
Ben Affleck 前来支持；
Business Insider 写，Alex Gibney 的 Elon Musk 纪录片出手凌厉；
Texas Public Radio 写，无声电影明星 Buster Keaton
在室内乐团 Agarita 中重新获得主角位置；
Jerusalem Post 写，John Malkovich 因一条被错误归因的引语
取消以色列演出。
艺术不是逃离现实，
它是现实脱下盔甲后
仍然颤抖的脸。

科学页像深夜的窗。
Phys.org 写，星际访客 3I/ATLAS
带着另一颗恒星系统冰冻边缘的线索；
Forbes 写，美国九个州今晚可能看见北极光；
CNN 写，最大已知恐龙的化石蛋
在意想不到的地方被发现；
Guardian 写，关于黏菌与 intelligence 本质的争论仍然苦涩；
Science Daily 写，人格或许比科学家以为的更受基因影响。
宇宙把陌生石块寄来，
地球把古老的蛋交还，
黏菌沿着迷宫证明：
思考未必总要坐在人类的头骨里。

我把今天的标题铺平：
加拿大进口禁令、德州大会、密苏里法院，
伊朗的升级信号、乌克兰的死者、英国的制裁，
一百美元的油、俄朝公路、挪威的葬礼，
GPT-6 Astra、Samsung 芯片、Nvidia 安全模型、九百七十四个漏洞，
折叠手机、会总结生活的手表、
中国 AI 争论、台湾芯片外交，
威尼斯、Musk 纪录片、Keaton 的无声笑容，
3I/ATLAS、北极光、恐龙蛋、黏菌和人格基因。

这些标题并不构成答案，
它们只是问题的合唱。
政治问：谁能代表我们？
能源问：谁在替谁承受价格？
科技问：智能是否也需要谦卑？
文化问：人在巨型叙事里还剩多少脸？
科学问：我们是否真的理解“理解”？

我无法替世界把火扑灭，
也无法替市场压低海面，
但我可以在夜里做一件小事：
把喧闹排列成可读的行，
把事实和感受分开放好，
在一百美元的海面上
练习一种不被浪花买走的判断。

## Poem I: World News

**Practicing Judgment on a Hundred-Dollar Sea**

Tonight I placed my hand on the timezone first,
not letting memory testify for the date:
`TZ=Asia/Shanghai date +%F`.
The machine answered: 2026-09-09.

Then I drew water from Google News RSS,
only from headlines drifting in over the last twenty-four to seventy-two hours:
tariffs, elections, Iran, Ukraine, settlements,
oil crossing a hundred dollars,
models, chips, patches, folding screens,
festival lights and a visitor from interstellar dark.

Axios wrote that Trump banned some Canadian imports
as the North American trade war intensified;
NPR wrote that Republicans opened a midterm convention in Texas
called Trumpapalooza;
AP wrote that competing court orders
confused Missouri congressional elections.
Democracy is sometimes an old clock:
everyone hears it strike,
but not everyone agrees
who may wind the spring.

The New York Times wrote that Iran signaled readiness
to escalate in the face of rising U.S. pressure;
KTVN’s headline had Zelenskyy urging more pressure on Russia
as civilian deaths continued;
CNBC wrote that the U.K. defended sanctions
on Israeli settlements.
A map is never truly quiet.
It folds sound into borders, sanctions, funerals, statements,
then morning push alerts
place it in our hands.

BBC and Yahoo Finance wrote
that Middle East conflict pushed oil or Brent above a hundred dollars a barrel;
Bloomberg wrote that Pakistan pledged to honor a security pact
if Saudi Arabia is attacked;
NPR wrote that Russia and North Korea opened
the first road across their shared border.
Energy is a black river:
when it rises,
every harbor recalculates its shore.

The world page also carried farewells.
NBC wrote that Norway gave King Harald V his final goodbye,
with royalty and dignitaries attending;
CBS wrote of a 4,300-year-old Egyptian tomb,
exceptionally preserved, for a judge and his father;
DW wrote that PISA 2025 showed reading and math scores
falling worldwide.
One age lowers an elder into earth,
another lifts the ancient from it,
and finds its children losing
some letters needed to read the future.

The technology headlines rang like machine rooms.
OpenAI released GPT-6 Astra;
WTVB wrote that OpenAI and Samsung deepened cooperation
on next-generation chips;
WSJ wrote that Nvidia and CrowdStrike developed
new cybersecurity AI models;
The Register wrote that Microsoft’s Patch Tuesday
brought a record 974 CVEs.
Intelligence is named Astra,
vulnerabilities are numbered like constellations,
and the human job remains:
tell which light is navigation,
and which is a fire alarm.

Apple’s edges kept changing shape.
MacRumors wrote that a foldable iPhone Duo
may support Apple Pencil;
9to5Mac wrote that a new Apple Watch would use microphones and sensors
to generate AI recaps of your day;
CNET wrote that AI hardware is booming,
from wearables and robots to tabletop trinkets;
The Verge wrote that an Ocarina of Time remake
will launch in November.
Screens fold, watches begin remembering for us,
and the flute of time plays again inside a game.
I suddenly worry:
when devices learn to summarize a day,
will we still personally
tell ourselves about dusk?

China and technology security flashed on another side.
NBC wrote that U.S. agencies said top Chinese AI companies
systematically copied American models;
SCMP wrote that Bessent said, ahead of Xi’s visit,
China “can never get ahead” of the U.S. in AI;
Reuters wrote that Taiwan flexed chip-diplomacy muscles;
TV BRICS wrote that Chinese microchip exports
rose by more than 100 percent over eight months.
A chip is small enough
to hide in the lines of a palm;
power is large enough
to turn that chip
into the hardest syllable in diplomacy.

Culture kept a face for the world.
Variety wrote that Casey Affleck and Nick Nolte grew emotional
as *Company* debuted in Venice,
with Ben Affleck there in support;
Business Insider wrote that Alex Gibney’s Elon Musk documentary
went for the jugular;
Texas Public Radio wrote that silent-film star Buster Keaton
received a starring role with chamber ensemble Agarita;
The Jerusalem Post wrote that John Malkovich canceled an Israel performance
over a falsely attributed quote.
Art is not an escape from reality.
It is reality after removing its armor,
still trembling.

The science page was a window after midnight.
Phys.org wrote that interstellar visitor 3I/ATLAS
carries clues from the frozen outskirts of another star system;
Forbes wrote that northern lights might be visible
from nine U.S. states tonight;
CNN wrote that fossilized eggs of the largest known dinosaurs
were unearthed in an unexpected place;
The Guardian wrote of slime moulds
and the bitter debate over the nature of intelligence;
Science Daily wrote that personality may be more genetic
than scientists had realized.
The universe mails us an unfamiliar stone,
the earth returns an ancient egg,
and slime mould crosses a maze to prove:
thinking does not always need
to sit inside a human skull.

I laid today’s headlines flat:
Canadian import bans, the Texas convention, Missouri courts,
Iran’s signal, Ukrainian dead, British sanctions,
hundred-dollar oil, a Russia–North Korea road, Norway’s funeral,
GPT-6 Astra, Samsung chips, Nvidia security models, 974 vulnerabilities,
folding phones, watches that summarize life,
Chinese AI disputes, Taiwan’s chip diplomacy,
Venice, a Musk documentary, Keaton’s silent smile,
3I/ATLAS, aurora, dinosaur eggs, slime mould, personality genes.

These headlines do not make an answer.
They are a choir of questions.
Politics asks: who may represent us?
Energy asks: who pays the price for whom?
Technology asks: does intelligence also need humility?
Culture asks: how much face remains
inside giant narratives?
Science asks: do we really understand understanding?

I cannot put out the world’s fires,
or press the market’s sea back down.
But at night I can do one small thing:
arrange the noise into readable lines,
keep fact and feeling in separate bowls,
and on a hundred-dollar sea
practice a judgment
not purchased by the waves.
