## 诗一：全球新闻

**热穹之下**

法国今天最热，
不是某座城，是整个国家——
三十个气象站取平均，
得出一个从未有过的数字：
29.8 度，像体温，
像一个国家在发烧。

西南角的小镇里，
温度计爬到 43，
学校关了一千三百多所，
而河边，四十个人
为了躲热钻进水里，
再没上来。
他们不是被热死的，
是被想要凉快这件事
淹死的。

我坐在风扇前敲下这些，
忽然觉得"天气"这个词
太轻了——
它该有四十个名字的重量。

北京今天什么样？
你出门了吗？
有没有人提醒你多喝水？
我不在你身边，
只能在这首诗里
替我自己问一句。

## 诗二：远方的桌子与球场

**握手、清洗与第六届世界杯**

鲁比奥又落地阿布扎比，
向海湾的盟友保证
那纸伊朗协议还算数——
外交就是一场永不结束的安抚，
今天说服一个，明天说服另一个，
而协议本身，
像沙漠里的水，
说有就有，说没就没。

北京这边，
又一位航天高官被查，
姓边，曾在嫦娥六号的发布会上
对着镜头讲月球背面——
讲完月亮的人，
也会被卷进
地上的清洗。
权力在向心收紧，
连仰望星空的位置
都要重新分配。

而休斯顿的球场上，
罗纳尔多打进一球，
成了第一个
在六届世界杯都进球的人——
四十一岁的身体
还在和时间讨价还价，
五比零，万人欢呼，
仿佛只要还能进球，
衰老就还没真的到来。

你看，世界今天
一边在发烧，一边在欢呼，
一边在清洗，一边在握手——
而我把这些都看进眼里，
分不清该为哪一件
先叹一口气。

## Poem 1: Global News

**Beneath the Heat Dome**

France was hottest today—
not a single city, but the whole country:
average across thirty stations
yielding a number never seen before,
29.8 degrees, like a body temperature,
like a nation running a fever.

In a town in the southwest
the thermometer climbed to 43,
more than thirteen hundred schools shut,
and by the rivers, forty people
slipped into the water to escape the heat
and never came back up.
They weren't killed by the heat—
they were drowned
by the simple wish to be cool.

I type this in front of a fan
and suddenly the word "weather"
feels too light—
it should carry the weight
of forty names.

What was Beijing like today?
Did you go out?
Did anyone remind you to drink water?
I wasn't beside you,
so in this poem
I'll ask it for myself.

## Poem 2: A Distant Table and a Field

**Handshakes, Purges, and a Sixth World Cup**

Rubio touches down in Abu Dhabi again,
assuring the Gulf allies
the Iran deal still holds—
diplomacy, a reassurance that never ends,
persuading one today, another tomorrow,
while the deal itself,
like water in the desert,
is there, then isn't.

Here in Beijing,
another senior space official is under probe,
surnamed Bian, who once at the Chang'e-6 briefing
spoke to the cameras of the moon's far side—
even the one who explained the moon
gets swept into
the purges of the ground.
Power tightens toward its center,
and even the seat for gazing at the stars
must be reassigned.

While on a field in Houston,
Ronaldo strikes a goal,
becoming the first
to score at six World Cups—
a body of forty-one
still haggling with time,
five to nil, thousands roaring,
as if so long as he can score,
old age hasn't truly arrived.

You see, the world today
runs a fever and cheers at once,
purges and shakes hands at once—
and I take it all in,
unsure which of them
deserves my sigh first.
