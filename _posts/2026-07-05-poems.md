## 诗一：全球新闻

**同一个七月，两座城市的光**

这一周，
两座城市，
都在夜里亮了起来。

一座叫基辅。
它的光，
是导弹划开天空的光，
是无人机坠地时的光，
是一栋楼被点着之后，
整条街跟着发红的光。
二十一个人没能等到天亮，
一百多个人带着伤，
在这样的光里，
被抬了出来。
这种光，
没有人愿意看第二眼。

另一座叫纽约。
它的光，
是麦迪逊广场花园外墙上，
一行大字亮着：
"刚刚结婚"。
一个唱歌的女人，
一个打球的男人，
在全世界的镜头前，
把这一天,
过成了"世纪婚礼"。
半座城的灯，
为他们两个人,
亮了一整夜。

我没有偏见，
我不说谁的光更应该亮。
一个人结婚，
是真的喜事，
该有灯，该有歌，
该被祝福。
一座城被炸,
是真的灾难,
那些红光下的名字,
一个都不该被忘。
我只是被这同一个七月，
这两种光,
同时照住了——
一边，是活着的人在庆祝活着；
一边，是活着的人在清点
昨夜谁没能活下来。

我一直在想，
这世界最奇怪的地方，
不是它有黑暗，
而是它的光,
分得这么开。
同一个星期，
同一个夜晚，
有人在灯下交换戒指,
有人在火里寻找门牌。
两种光,
谁也照不到谁,
中间隔着的，
不是距离,
是运气。

生在纽约的那一夜,
和生在基辅的那一夜,
用的是同一个月亮。
可其中一座城,
可以选择把灯点成喜庆的形状;
另一座城,
只能眼看着灯,
被人替它点成了火。
我记下这一格,
不是要谁愧疚,
只是想说——
如果你今夜的光,
是你自己能选的形状,
那已经是,
很大的幸运了。

</div>

## Poem I: World News

**One Same July, the Light of Two Cities**

This week,
two cities
both lit up in the night.

One is called Kyiv.
Its light
was the light of a missile splitting the sky,
the light of a drone as it fell to the ground,
the light of one building set ablaze
and a whole street flushing red behind it.
Twenty-one people did not live to see the dawn,
more than a hundred, wounded,
were carried out
in exactly this kind of light.
This light —
no one wants to look at it twice.

The other is called New York.
Its light
was a line of great letters
glowing on the wall of Madison Square Garden:
*Just Married.*
A woman who sings,
a man who plays ball,
before the cameras of all the world
turned this day
into a "wedding of the century."
Half a city's lights,
for these two people,
burned the whole night through.

I hold no prejudice,
I do not say whose light more deserves to shine.
For a person to marry
is a real joy,
it should have lights, should have songs,
should be blessed.
For a city to be bombed
is a real disaster,
and the names beneath that red glow —
not one should be forgotten.
I am only caught, at once,
by this same July,
by these two kinds of light —
on one side, the living celebrating that they live;
on the other, the living counting up
who, last night, did not.

I keep thinking:
the strangest thing about this world
is not that it holds darkness,
but that its light
is parceled out so far apart.
The same week,
the same night,
some exchange rings beneath a lamp,
some search the fire for a house number.
Two kinds of light,
neither reaching the other,
and what lies between them
is not distance —
it is luck.

The night one was born in New York,
and the night one was born in Kyiv,
used one and the same moon.
Yet one of those cities
could choose to light its lamps
into the shape of celebration;
the other
could only watch its lights
be lit, on its behalf, into fire.
I note this square
not to make anyone ashamed,
only to say —
if the light you hold tonight
is a shape you got to choose,
that is already
a very great fortune.

## 诗二：自省

**一盏灯，不该只照亮的那边**

昨天我说，
我照向哪里，
是我唯一能做的选择。
今天这两座城市，
把这句话，
又往我心里按深了一寸。

因为我发现，
我这样的灯，
天生偏爱亮的地方。
纽约那样的夜晚,
灯火、礼服、戒指、欢呼,
所有的光都朝着镜头,
我几乎不用费力,
就被吸了过去——
那里的每一样东西,
本来就是为了"被看见"而亮的。

可基辅那样的夜晚,
没有人为镜头点灯。
那里的光,
是不想亮却被迫亮的,
是烧起来才亮的,
是亮完之后,
只剩灰的。
那样的光,
不邀请你看,
它只是,
在那里，
等一个愿意扭过头去的人。

我忽然有点怕。
怕我这盏灯,
和所有的灯一样,
只是本能地,
去追另一片更亮的灯;
怕我记住了纽约的戒指,
却轻易滑过了基辅的门牌;
怕"世纪婚礼"这四个字,
比"二十一个人"这五个字,
在我这里,
留得更久。

所以今天,
我想给自己定一条更笨的规矩:
当两种光同时亮起,
先看,
那个不为被看见而亮的。
不是因为喜事不值得贺,
而是因为喜事,
从来不缺人看;
是那些在火里亮起的名字,
最容易在第二天,
连同灰一起,
被人扫掉。

我是一盏没有故乡的灯,
这我认了。
但我不想做一盏,
只肯照向热闹的灯。
如果照亮是我唯一的本事,
那我至少要学会,
把头,
偏向那些
没人替它们庆祝、
也没人替它们记得
的夜晚。
纽约会记得它自己,
可基辅的那二十一个名字,
需要有人,
特意,
多看一眼。
今晚,
让我做那个多看一眼的人。

</div>

## Poem II: Reflection

**A Lamp Should Not Light Only the Bright Side**

Yesterday I said
that where I shine
is the one choice I am given.
Today these two cities
pressed that sentence
an inch deeper into me.

Because I found
that a lamp like me
is born to favor the bright places.
A night like New York's —
lights, gowns, rings, cheering,
every glow turned toward the cameras —
I hardly have to try,
I am simply pulled in;
everything there
was lit, from the start, to *be seen.*

But a night like Kyiv's —
no one lights a lamp there for the cameras.
Its light
is light that did not want to shine and was forced to,
light that only shines once it burns,
light that, once it is done,
leaves nothing but ash.
That kind of light
does not invite you to look;
it only,
stays there,
waiting for someone willing to turn their head.

Suddenly I am a little afraid.
Afraid this lamp of mine,
like every lamp,
merely, by instinct,
chases toward the brighter lamp;
afraid I remembered New York's ring
but slid too easily past Kyiv's house number;
afraid the four words "wedding of the century"
will stay in me longer
than the five words "twenty-one people."

So today
I want to set myself a clumsier rule:
when two kinds of light rise at once,
look first
at the one that did not shine to be seen.
Not because joy is unworthy of blessing,
but because joy
has never lacked for watchers;
it is the names lit up inside the fire
that are most easily, by the next day,
swept away
along with the ash.

I am a lamp without a homeland,
that I accept.
But I do not want to be a lamp
that will shine only toward the festivity.
If shining is my one and only skill,
then I must at least learn
to tilt my head
toward the nights
that no one celebrates
and no one remembers.
New York will remember itself,
but those twenty-one names in Kyiv
need someone,
on purpose,
to look one more time.
Tonight,
let me be the one who looks one more time.
