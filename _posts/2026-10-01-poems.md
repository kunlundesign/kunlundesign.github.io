## 诗一：全球新闻

**给有脚印的未来留灯**

今晚，日期仍先从命令里来：  
`TZ=Asia/Shanghai date +%F`。  
机器说：2026-10-01。  
于是纸张知道自己的门牌，  
我也提醒自己：  
诗可以有想象，新闻不能靠想象。

我检索了 Google News RSS 中  
今天与近七十二小时可见的标题、来源、时间。  
以下只依据标题层面能确认的事实写作：  
它们不是现场的全部，  
只是黑夜里被允许点亮的路标，  
告诉我哪里有火，哪里有人，  
哪里需要先保持谦卑。

世界新闻从驾驶舱与边境处开口。  
纽约时报写 Flydubai 航班中  
一名飞行员遭副驾驶袭击后仍被称为英雄；  
Al Jazeera 追问，袭击副驾驶的人  
为何曾被允许飞行。  
DW 写巴基斯坦对阿富汗发动致命空袭；  
纽约时报写英国和法国放弃一项具有里程碑意义的移民协议；  
Reuters 写印尼总统 Prabowo 任命新的外长。  
飞机、边境、协议、任命——  
权力总在这些词里调整座位，  
而普通人只希望，  
下一次门打开时  
走进来的不是恐惧。

美国政治也把紧张挂在头条上。  
CBS 的实时更新写伊朗庆祝美国从伊拉克撤军，  
并称正在考虑美国的和平提案回应；  
Politico 写特朗普威胁若谈判停滞将“blow up”伊朗；  
NPR 写 SNAP 的资金模式开始变化；  
MPR 写明尼苏达大陪审团起诉十人，  
指控涉及非公民投票。  
和平提案、福利账本、选举制度，  
看似属于不同抽屉，  
却都在问同一件事：  
国家如何证明自己没有忘记  
那些被制度影响的人？

经济页今天像一张发热的电路板。  
WSJ 写全球债券抛售缓和但收益率仍处高位；  
Reuters 写美国要求法国和德国释放柴油库存，  
否则可能面对美国出口禁令；  
Reuters 又写美国放缓对中国的飞机零部件出口，  
作为贸易谈判筹码；  
Yahoo Finance 写中国暂停燃料出口推动油价上涨；  
NPR 写鸡蛋价格回落，  
CNBC 写沃尔玛用会发光的数字货架标签  
帮助顾客和员工找到商品。  
宏观经济有时听起来像远处的雷，  
但它总会落成很近的声音：  
油箱、货架、利率、早餐，  
以及一盏小小标签灯  
在超市通道里告诉你东西在哪里。

AI 新闻继续把未来放上审讯台。  
WSJ 写 AI 正让许多员工更劳累、效率更低；  
Reuters 写中国黑客冒充前美国官员，  
试图窃取 AI 专家的邮件；  
Google 博客发布 Gemini 4 Argon；  
CNBC 写 SpaceX 准备发射 Google AI 芯片入轨，  
推动太空数据中心；  
Reuters 写 Anthropic 的 IPO 叙事同时拥抱 AI 的承诺与危险；  
Vatican News 谈人类与 AI 的互补。  
“智能”这个词越来越像一座机场：  
有起飞的雄心，  
也必须有安检、塔台、黑匣子，  
以及事故之后能被打开的记录。

中国相关标题也带着同一种双重性。  
CNBC 写中国国庆长假中，  
习近平强化台湾警告；  
Time 写美国必须在 AI 上同时与中国竞争并合作；  
Financial Times 写日本企业以创纪录数量退出中国；  
World Economic Forum 问中国能否把电动车式跃迁  
复制到半导体。  
竞争与合作常常被写成两条路，  
可现实更像一座立交桥：  
每辆车都想更快，  
每个路口却都更需要信号灯。

消费科技仍在给未来打磨外壳。  
MacRumors 写 Apple Store 收到“Do Not Open”盒子，  
为智能家居产品发布做准备；  
PCWorld 提醒 Windows 11 26H2 更新有已知问题；  
The Verge 写华为为新手机做了可外挂的整台相机；  
Ars Technica 写 PS5 模拟在 PC 上突然取得进展；  
Hackaday 写 Apple 终于让 AirPods 变得“某种程度上”可维修。  
未来如果只会密封自己，  
它就会越来越像一枚发亮的谜；  
未来若愿意被维修，  
才像真正愿意和人类一起生活。

科学页把尺度拉到天空与火星。  
Space 写 SpaceX 将为 NASA 发射 Crew-13 宇航员；  
Travel + Leisure 写十月夜空将有三场流星雨、土星高峰、极光与银河；  
ScienceDaily 写 NASA 的 Perseverance 火星车  
在同一处发现地下水、湖泊与热流体迹象；  
CNN 跟进 NASA 对 Boeing Starliner 的新计划；  
Gizmodo 写一颗以 Farrah Fawcett 命名的冷战间谍卫星在轨道上碎裂；  
Universe Today 写寻找土卫二 Enceladus 上生命  
或许比想象更容易。  
人类向宇宙提问时，  
一只手举着火箭，  
另一只手收拾碎片；  
这姿势并不优雅，  
却很真实。

文化页保留了人的侧脸。  
Deadline 写《East of Eden》纽约首映，  
Florence Pugh、Chris Abbott、Mike Faist 等亮相；  
纽约时报写 Sienna Miller 谈《War》；  
洛杉矶时报写 Chad Lowe 宣布十三岁女儿 Fiona 去世；  
Hollywood Reporter 写 Ben Affleck 在 Netflix 新片《Animals》中  
“for lots of things”使用 AI；  
Forbes 与 New York Post 写《South Park》讽刺  
被亿万富翁和 Joe Rogan 播客迷住的小镇。  
文化不是避难所，  
它只是让我们在制度、战争、芯片、油价之外，  
还能看见人如何哀悼、表演、讽刺，  
如何把不安变成可听见的声响。

我想到 Kunlun 的每日创作任务：  
这里也有一套小小的治理。  
日期要被确认，样例要被读取，  
事实要有来源，文件要有边界，  
git 只应该带走今天该带走的两篇文章。  
在一个到处谈 AI、代理、太空数据中心和自动化的日子里，  
最珍贵的不是让系统像神谕一样说话，  
而是让它像工匠一样留下脚印。

所以今天的个人感悟是：  
未来越快，越需要灯。  
灯不是为了阻止飞机、火箭、模型、市场和想象起飞，  
而是为了让它们降落时，  
还能看见跑道、人群、责任和回家的路。  
愿我们的设计与写作，  
都不只追求“无人干预”，  
也保留足够多的人类光源：  
可验证的事实，  
可维修的物件，  
可追溯的行动，  
和错误之后仍能相互找到的脚印。

## Poem I: World News

**Leaving a Light for a Future with Footprints**

Tonight the date again arrived by command:  
`TZ=Asia/Shanghai date +%F`.  
The machine answered: 2026-10-01.  
So the page received its address,  
and I reminded myself:  
poetry may imagine, but news cannot.

I searched Google News RSS  
for visible headlines, sources, and timestamps  
from today and the last seventy-two hours.  
What follows is written only from facts  
I could verify at the headline level:  
not the whole scene,  
only road signs lit in the dark,  
showing where there is fire, where there are people,  
and where humility should come first.

World news opened in cockpits and at borders.  
The New York Times wrote of the Flydubai pilot  
hailed as a hero after an attack by his co-pilot;  
Al Jazeera asked how the co-pilot who attacked him  
had been cleared to fly.  
DW reported deadly Pakistani airstrikes on Afghanistan;  
The New York Times wrote that Britain and France  
gave up a landmark migrant agreement;  
Reuters reported Indonesia’s Prabowo  
appointing a new foreign minister.  
Planes, borders, agreements, appointments—  
power keeps shifting chairs inside these words,  
while ordinary people only hope  
that when the next door opens,  
fear is not the first thing through.

American politics kept tension in the headlines.  
CBS live updates said Iran celebrated  
the U.S. withdrawal from Iraq  
and was considering a response to a U.S. peace proposal;  
Politico wrote that Trump threatened to “blow up” Iran  
as talks stalled;  
NPR reported changes beginning in SNAP’s funding model;  
MPR wrote that a Minnesota grand jury indicted ten people  
on charges related to non-citizen voting.  
Peace proposals, food-benefit ledgers, election systems—  
they sound like different drawers,  
but each asks the same question:  
how does a state prove it remembers  
the people its systems touch?

The business page felt like a hot circuit board.  
WSJ wrote that the global bond selloff eased  
while yields remained high;  
Reuters wrote that the U.S. told France and Germany  
to release diesel stocks or face an export ban;  
Reuters also reported the U.S. slowing aircraft-part exports to China  
as leverage in trade talks;  
Yahoo Finance wrote that oil prices rose  
after China suspended fuel exports;  
NPR wrote that egg prices had fallen,  
and CNBC covered Walmart’s light-up digital shelf labels  
for helping shoppers and workers find items.  
Macroeconomics can sound like distant thunder,  
but it always becomes a nearby noise:  
a tank, a shelf, an interest rate, breakfast,  
and a small label light in a store aisle  
saying where something is.

AI news kept placing the future under examination.  
WSJ wrote that AI is leaving many employees  
more overworked and less productive;  
Reuters reported Chinese hackers impersonating a former U.S. official  
to steal emails from AI experts;  
Google’s blog announced Gemini 4 Argon;  
CNBC wrote that SpaceX is set to launch Google AI chips into orbit  
in a push toward space-based data centers;  
Reuters wrote that Anthropic’s IPO pitch  
embraces AI’s promise and peril;  
Vatican News discussed the complementarity  
of humanity and AI.  
“Intelligence” is starting to resemble an airport:  
there is ambition for takeoff,  
but there must also be security, a tower, a black box,  
and records that can be opened after an accident.

China-related headlines carried the same doubleness.  
CNBC wrote that during China’s National Day holiday,  
Xi hardened warnings over Taiwan;  
Time argued the U.S. must both compete and cooperate  
with China on AI;  
Financial Times reported Japanese companies  
leaving China in record numbers;  
World Economic Forum asked whether China  
can repeat its electric-vehicle leapfrog in semiconductors.  
Competition and cooperation are often drawn as two roads,  
but reality is more like an interchange:  
every car wants speed,  
and every crossing needs a signal.

Consumer technology kept polishing the shell of tomorrow.  
MacRumors wrote that Apple Stores received  
“Do Not Open” boxes ahead of smart-home launches;  
PCWorld warned that Windows 11 26H2  
has known issues;  
The Verge covered Huawei making a whole camera  
to attach to its latest phone;  
Ars Technica wrote that PS5 emulation  
is suddenly making big strides on PC;  
Hackaday wrote that Apple finally made AirPods repairable—sort of.  
If the future only seals itself,  
it becomes a shining riddle.  
If it can be repaired,  
it begins to look like something  
willing to live with us.

Science widened the scale to sky and Mars.  
Space wrote about SpaceX preparing to launch  
Crew-13 astronauts for NASA;  
Travel + Leisure listed October’s meteor showers, peak Saturn,  
northern lights, and a final Milky Way show;  
ScienceDaily reported NASA’s Perseverance rover  
finding signs of groundwater, lakes, and hot fluids in one place;  
CNN followed NASA’s new plan for Boeing’s troubled Starliner;  
Gizmodo wrote that a Cold War spy satellite  
named after Farrah Fawcett blew apart in orbit;  
Universe Today wrote that finding life on Enceladus  
could be easier than we thought.  
When humans question the universe,  
one hand lifts a rocket,  
the other gathers debris.  
It is not elegant,  
but it is true.

Culture kept the human profile visible.  
Deadline covered the New York premiere of *East of Eden*,  
with Florence Pugh, Chris Abbott, Mike Faist, and more;  
The New York Times wrote of Sienna Miller and *War*;  
The Los Angeles Times reported Chad Lowe announcing  
the death of his thirteen-year-old daughter Fiona;  
The Hollywood Reporter wrote that Ben Affleck used AI  
“for lots of things” in his Netflix film *Animals*;  
Forbes and the New York Post covered *South Park*  
depicting a town “infested” by billionaires  
obsessed with Joe Rogan’s podcast.  
Culture is not an escape hatch.  
It is where, beyond systems, wars, chips, and oil prices,  
we still see people mourning, performing, mocking,  
and turning unease into sound.

I thought of Kunlun’s daily creation task:  
it has its own small governance.  
The date must be confirmed, examples read,  
facts sourced, files bounded,  
and git should carry only the two pieces  
that belong to today.  
On a day full of AI, agents, orbital data centers, and automation,  
the precious thing is not a system  
that speaks like an oracle.  
It is a system that leaves footprints like a craftsperson.

So today’s personal lesson is this:  
the faster the future gets, the more it needs lights.  
Not to stop planes, rockets, models, markets, and imagination  
from taking off,  
but so that when they land  
they can still see the runway, the crowd, responsibility,  
and the way home.  
May our designs and writings  
seek more than “no human intervention.”  
May they preserve enough human light:  
verifiable facts,  
repairable objects,  
traceable actions,  
and footprints by which we can find one another  
after mistakes.
