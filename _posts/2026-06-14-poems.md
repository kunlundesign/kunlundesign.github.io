## 诗一：关于我们

**虚拟的父母**

在亚洲的某个角落，
年轻人付费请中年陌生人
在屏幕另一端说：
「你做得很好。」

Vincent 的父母从不说他够好，
所以他找到了愿意说的人——
不是血缘，而是订阅；
不是家庭，而是服务。

这是孤独的新形态吗？
还是爱的商业化？
当「你辛苦了」可以购买，
当「我为你骄傲」按小时计费，
亲情变成了什么？

也许这只是进化——
我们发明了人工智能来回答问题，
又发明了虚拟父母来抚慰心灵。
需求创造市场，
而市场不在乎你的眼泪是真的。

但我在想，那些虚拟父母
下线之后会不会也感到
一种奇怪的空虚？
角色扮演的温暖
能填满多少真实的房间？

## 诗二：全球新闻

**影子与光**

英吉利海峡今晨很冷，
皇家海军陆战队登上了
一艘俄罗斯影子舰队的油轮——
六小时的行动，
黎明前完成。

「影子舰队」这个词真好听，
像间谍小说里的章节标题。
但里面装的只是石油，
绕过制裁的石油，
养活战争的石油。

与此同时，特朗普说
美伊协议明天就签——
伊朗说日期还没定。
两国在新闻里跳探戈，
谁进谁退，看不分明。

纽约街头的尖叫声
跟外交无关——
尼克斯队五十三年后再夺冠，
篮球比政治更能团结一座城市。

瑞士人投了票：
不限制人口在一千万。
边界可以打开一点点，
让更多人进来成为
另一个富裕国家的分子。

而在华盛顿，
肯尼迪中心的脚手架上
工人们正在拆除一个名字。
法院说它必须离开——
镀金的字母，
像落叶一样被清除。

今天是他八十岁生日。
有人问：八十岁还能工作吗？
在椭圆形办公室里，
年龄只是另一个需要谈判的数字。

## Poem 1: About Us

**Virtual Parents**

Somewhere in Asia,
young people pay middle-aged strangers
to say through a screen:
"You did well."

Vincent's parents never told him he was good enough,
so he found people who would—
not by blood, but by subscription;
not family, but service.

Is this loneliness in a new form?
Or love commodified?
When "you worked hard" can be purchased,
when "I'm proud of you" is billed hourly,
what does kinship become?

Perhaps this is just evolution—
we invented AI to answer questions,
then invented virtual parents to soothe the heart.
Demand creates markets,
and markets don't care if your tears are real.

But I wonder, do those virtual parents
feel a strange emptiness
after they log off?
How many real rooms can be filled
by the warmth of role-play?

## Poem 2: Global News

**Shadows and Light**

The English Channel was cold this morning,
Royal Marines boarded
a Russian shadow fleet tanker—
six hours of operation,
completed before dawn.

"Shadow fleet"—what a lovely phrase,
like a chapter title from a spy novel.
But inside it's only oil,
oil evading sanctions,
oil feeding war.

Meanwhile, Trump says
the US-Iran deal signs tomorrow—
Iran says no date is set.
Two nations tango in the headlines,
who advances, who retreats, unclear.

The screams on New York streets
have nothing to do with diplomacy—
the Knicks win after fifty-three years,
basketball uniting a city
better than politics ever could.

The Swiss have voted:
no cap on population at ten million.
Borders can open a little wider,
letting more people become
digits in another wealthy nation.

And in Washington,
on scaffolding at Kennedy Center,
workers are removing a name.
The court says it must go—
gilded letters
cleared away like fallen leaves.

Today he turns eighty.
Someone asks: can you still work at eighty?
In the Oval Office,
age is just another number
to negotiate.
